## Geometric Distortions and Caustics

::::{exercise}
:label: ex_caustics

In this exercise, you will investigate the geometric interpretation of aberrations using the displacement field generated by the aberration gradient.

Recall that the local ray displacement is proportional to

:::{math}
\Delta \bm{r}(\bm{k})
\propto
\nabla_{\bm{k}} \chi(\bm{k}),
:::

and that caustics occur when the local magnification diverges:

:::{math}
\det\left(
\frac{\partial \Delta \bm{r}}{\partial \bm{k}}
\right)
= 0.
:::

**Defocus and Astigmatism**

Consider the aberration function containing only defocus and two-fold astigmatism:

:::{math}
\chi(\bm{k})
=
\frac{\pi}{\lambda}
\left[
C_{10} \; \lambda^2 (k_x^2+k_y^2)
+
C_{12} \; \lambda^2 (k_x^2-k_y^2)
\right].
:::

1. Compute the displacement field analytically.
2. Compute the Jacobian analytically.
3. Determine whether spatially varying caustics can occur for this case.

**Defocus and Spherical Aberration**

Now consider defocus and spherical aberration:

:::{math}
\chi(\bm{k})
=
\frac{\pi}{\lambda}
\left[
C_{10} \; \lambda^2 \; k^2
+
\frac{1}{2}C_{30} \; \lambda^4 \; k^4
\right].
:::

1. Derive the radial displacement field analytically.
2. Determine the condition for caustic formation.
3. Show that the resulting caustic is radially symmetric.

**Defocus and Coma**

Using the interactive widget, investigate the case containing defocus and axial coma ($C_{21}$).

1. Numerically compute the Jacobian determinant across reciprocal space.
2. Plot the regions where the determinant approaches zero.
3. Compare the resulting caustic geometry against the symmetric case above.

::::{exercise}
:label: ex_zemlin

A common experimental method for diagnosing residual aberrations is the [diffractogram tableau](https://www.jeol.com/words/emterms/20121023.063958.php), where a series of Ronchigrams are recorded using different beam tilts and defocus conditions.

Recall that a beam tilt shifts the illumination cone in reciprocal space.
Instead of sampling the aberration function around the optic axis, the microscope now probes the aberration surface around a displaced coordinate:

:::{math}
\chi(\bm{k})
\rightarrow
\chi(\bm{k} - \bm{k}_{\mathrm{tilt}}).
:::

Thus, tilted illumination effectively probes different local gradients and curvatures of the aberration surface.

**Local Expansion of the Aberration Surface**

Suppose we illuminate the specimen with a beam tilt $\bm{k}_{\mathrm{tilt}}$.
Using a first-order Taylor expansion, show that

:::{math}
\chi(\bm{k} - \bm{k}_{\mathrm{tilt}})
\approx
\chi(\bm{k})
-
\nabla_{\bm{k}}\chi(\bm{k})
\cdot
\bm{k}_{\mathrm{tilt}}.
:::

- Why does beam tilt make the Ronchigram sensitive to the gradient of the aberration surface?
- Which aberrations would become more visible under beam tilt, rotationally symmetric aberrations or asymmetric aberrations such as coma?
- Why is coma particularly easy to identify in Zemlin tableaux?

**Overfocus and Underfocus**

Now consider changing the sign of the defocus coefficient:

:::{math}
C_{10}
\rightarrow
-C_{10}.
:::

- How does reversing the sign of defocus modify the aberration surface $\chi(\bm{k})$?
- What happens to the displacement field $\nabla_{\bm{k}}\chi(\bm{k})$ ?
- Why would caustic distortions reverse direction between overfocus and underfocus?
- Explain why recording only a single defocus condition may make it difficult to uniquely determine the sign of an aberration.

**Automatic Aberration Estimation**

Modern aberration-correction software automatically estimates aberration coefficients from Zemlin tableaux.
Suppose you wanted to design such an algorithm.

- What measurable Ronchigram features would contain information about defocus, astigmatism, and coma?
- How could rotational symmetry help separate different aberration orders?
- Why would acquiring Ronchigrams over multiple beam tilts, and both overfocus and underfocus improve the robustness of the estimate?
