## Aplanatic Aberrations

::::{exercise}
:label: ex_aplanatic_aberrations

Using the interactive aberration widget from @aplanatic_page, investigate how residual aberrations limit the optimal convergence semi-angle of a STEM probe.

**Instructions:**
1. Introduce a significant combination of low-order aberrations (for example defocus, astigmatism, and coma).
2. Start from a very large convergence semi-angle.
3. Gradually reduce the convergence semi-angle until the real-space probe becomes as small and localized as possible.
4. Examine the corresponding phase variation of the aberration function across the probe-forming aperture.

**Questions:**
1. Why does increasing the convergence angle not always improve the probe size?
2. Estimate the maximum phase variation across the aperture at the optimum probe condition.
3. Compare your observation against the commonly used phase-tolerance criterion

:::{math}
|\chi(\bm{k})| \lesssim \frac{\pi}{4}.
:::

## Geometric Distortions and Caustics

::::{exercise}
:label: ex_caustics

In this exercise, you will investigate the geometric interpretation of aberrations using the displacement field generated by the aberration gradient.

Recall that the local ray displacement is proportional to

:::{math}
\Delta \bm{r}(\bm{k})
\propto
\nabla_{\bm{k}} \chi(\bm{k}),
:::

and that caustics occur when the local magnification diverges:

:::{math}
\det\left(
\frac{\partial \Delta \bm{r}}{\partial \bm{k}}
\right)
= 0.
:::

**Defocus and Astigmatism**

Consider the aberration function containing only defocus and two-fold astigmatism:

:::{math}
\chi(\bm{k})
=
\frac{\pi}{\lambda}
\left[
C_{10} \; \lambda^2 (k_x^2+k_y^2)
+
C_{12} \; \lambda^2 (k_x^2-k_y^2)
\right].
:::

1. Compute the displacement field analytically.
2. Compute the Jacobian analytically.
3. Determine whether spatially varying caustics can occur for this case.

**Defocus and Spherical Aberration**

Now consider defocus and spherical aberration:

:::{math}
\chi(\bm{k})
=
\frac{\pi}{\lambda}
\left[
C_{10} \; \lambda^2 \; k^2
+
\frac{1}{2}C_{30} \; \lambda^4 \; k^4
\right].
:::

1. Derive the radial displacement field analytically.
2. Determine the condition for caustic formation.
3. Show that the resulting caustic is radially symmetric.

**Defocus and Coma**

Using the interactive widget, investigate the case containing defocus and axial coma ($C_{21}$).

1. Numerically compute the Jacobian determinant across reciprocal space.
2. Plot the regions where the determinant approaches zero.
3. Compare the resulting caustic geometry against the symmetric case above.

## Ronchigram Aberration Correction

::::{exercise}
:label: ex_ronchigram_correction

In this exercise, you will practice manual aberration correction using an interactive Ronchigram simulator.
Navigate to [ronchigram.com](https://ronchigram.com/vasco/) and select **"Easy Training"** mode.

**Instructions:**

Using the Ronchigram appearance as feedback:

1. Adjust the aberration coefficients to minimize distortions.
2. Try to maximize the region of smooth, interpretable contrast.
3. Continue iterating until the probe appears well corrected.

**Questions:**

1. Which aberrations were easiest to identify visually?
2. Which aberrations were most difficult to correct?
3. What Ronchigram features were most useful for identifying residual aberrations?
